## Why this exists

OpenSubs is released to the public under the **AGPL-3.0-or-later**. The
AGPL guarantees that anyone who receives the software may use, modify and
redistribute it freely.

Apple’s App Store terms pull the other way: they limit installation to the
recipient’s own devices, cap the number of them, and impose digital rights
management. Those two sets of terms cannot both hold over the same code, which
is why VLC was removed from the App Store in 2011 after a contributor objected.

Today the project can be in both places at once because every line of it is the
Maintainer’s own. A licence binds the people a copyright holder grants it to;
it does not bind the copyright holder. So the same code can be AGPL to the
public and, separately, shipped by the Project Owner under Apple’s terms. That is
ordinary dual licensing.

**That stops working the moment a contribution by someone else is merged
without this agreement.** The contributor’s copyright in their own lines would
be licensed to the project under the AGPL alone, and the project could no
longer ship a build containing them under Apple’s terms. The only remedies then
are to not merge, or to strip those lines out of every store build — and
retroactive fixes mean contacting every past contributor, which fails the first
time one cannot be reached.

This agreement removes that problem once, at no cost to you.
